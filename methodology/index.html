Methodology

We acquire official records, validate their structure, normalize vehicle identities carefully, and calculate only what the evidence supports.

Vehicle identity

Year, make, and model matches are exact unless an explicit, sourced alias exists. We do not use fuzzy matching to merge similar vehicle names. Unresolved identities do not publish.

Complaint signal

A complaint count is the number of records returned by the NHTSA complaint API for a year, make, and model. It is not a failure rate because the dataset does not provide a matching population or exposure denominator. Reports are not independently verified facts about every vehicle.

Component mentions and issue topics

One complaint may carry multiple NHTSA component labels. We split and count those source labels, preserve them in page data, and map them to an inspectable issue taxonomy. Component totals can therefore exceed complaint totals. Topic shares use all component mentions in the same snapshot as their denominator.

Recalls, investigations, and communications

Recall summaries are normalized from official campaign records. A model-year match does not confirm whether a particular VIN is affected or repaired; owners should use NHTSA's VIN lookup. Investigation and manufacturer-communication archives use exact vehicle selection and retain official action or document identifiers.

History and comparisons

We never backfill observations we did not capture. The first snapshot is a baseline; change and trend language appears only after comparable snapshots exist. Adjacent-year comparisons remain unavailable until both sides pass the same quality checks.
